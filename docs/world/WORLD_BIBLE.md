# Whisker Realm World Bible

This file implements step 11 of `docs/TBH_GAME_SPEC_1_TO_100.md` and extends `project_main_setting.md` without replacing it.

## Core Premise

**Whisker Realm** is the fantasy-facing identity of **TBH: Task Bar Hero**. Its central world is **Purralia**, a warm medieval fantasy continent suspended on the **Great Weave**. Every life, road, memory, spell, and kingdom is held together by living strands called **Yarnthread**.

The world was first woven by **The First Weaver**, a mythic creator figure remembered as a colossal ancient cat. The player enters the story when the Great Weave begins to loosen again under the pressure of **The Unravel**.

## The Great Weave

The Great Weave is not only cosmology. It is the game world's visual and systemic metaphor:

- Roads are "threads" between safe places.
- Progression is "reweaving" damaged regions.
- Crafting is "stitching" better gear.
- Healing is "mending" torn Yarnthread.
- Corruption is "fraying" or "unraveling" the weave.

This makes every system easier to keep on-theme: combat, loot, crafting, regions, sound, and UI can all speak the same design language.

## Yarnthread

Yarnthread is the life-thread running through the world. It has three practical design roles:

- **Narrative:** it explains why Purralia can be damaged and repaired.
- **Gameplay:** it justifies upgrades, runes, magic schools, and region restoration.
- **Visual/Sound:** it supports thread, cloth, bell, purr, loom, stitch, and chime motifs.

## The First Weaver

The First Weaver is the ancient source of the Great Weave. The First Weaver should remain mythic during early content:

- Mentioned in songs, nursery rhymes, shrine carvings, and tutorial narration.
- Never fully explained in the first region.
- Used as a soft mystery that can grow with future chapters.

## The Unravel

The Unravel is the core threat. It should feel serious but not grimdark.

The Unravel does not simply "destroy"; it loosens meaning:

- Colors fade.
- Roads stop connecting.
- memories become fuzzy.
- monsters appear as knots, lint, shadow-thread, and tangled fur.
- safe places become quiet in an uncomfortable way.

The player fantasy is not conquest. The fantasy is **restoration**: the party defeats threats by holding the world together and reweaving damaged places.

## Player Role

The player is the **Threadbound**, a new leader connected strongly enough to Yarnthread to gather cat knights and guide them through unstable regions. The Threadbound does not need to be a visible avatar in the first prototype. The role can be expressed through taskbar commands, party upgrades, and story text.

## Playable Culture

The playable heroes are **Felikin**, catfolk organized into clans. Each clan should stay readable at a glance:

- Maincoon / Ironmane: protective knights.
- Siamheart / Voicecasters: vocal magic casters.
- Persephone / Silkpaw: elegant support nobles.
- Bengalis / Wildstripe: high-energy ambush fighters.
- Ragdollan / Cloudfur: gentle healers.
- Shorthold / Emberpaw: balanced adventurers.

## Non-Playable Peoples

Other peoples exist to make Purralia feel lived-in:

- Woolfolk: weavers, merchants, crafters.
- Houndsworn: honorable rivals and uneasy allies.
- Tinkerkin: builders, inventors, base-upgrade helpers.
- Mothkin: Silkspiredale mystics and keepers of old thread secrets.
- Ratterlings: morally gray traders, spies, scavengers, and tricksters.

## World Promise

The world promise is: **a cute party of cat knights repairs a soft medieval fantasy world before a quiet cosmic unraveling pulls it apart.**

Every feature should ask whether it supports that promise.
