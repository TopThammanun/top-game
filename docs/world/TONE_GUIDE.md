# Tone Guide

This file implements step 20 of `docs/TBH_GAME_SPEC_1_TO_100.md`.

## Tone Statement

TBH should feel like a cozy medieval fantasy idle RPG where cute cat knights protect a soft world from a real but gently presented cosmic threat.

The emotional target is:

> Warm, playful, tactile, lightly funny, and quietly heroic.

## What The Game Is

- Cozy, but not empty.
- Cute, but not weightless.
- Funny, but not parody-only.
- Idle-friendly, but not boring.
- Heroic, but not harsh.
- Magical, but grounded in thread, cloth, bells, warmth, and sound.

## What The Game Is Not

- Not dark fantasy.
- Not cynical.
- Not noisy for the sake of noise.
- Not a hard-pressure monetization game.
- Not a lore dump that interrupts idle flow.
- Not a meme-first cat game where the world stops mattering.

## Humor Rules

Use soft wordplay and small situational jokes. Humor can come from:

- overly serious knightly titles for small cozy tasks
- market gossip
- dramatic descriptions of tiny inconveniences
- gentle clan rivalry
- taskbar tooltips

Avoid jokes that make The Unravel feel fake. The threat can be whimsical in presentation, but its effect on Purralia must matter.

## Threat Rules

The Unravel should create unease through absence and looseness, not gore or shock.

Use:

- faded color
- missing sounds
- thread knots
- repeated signs
- soft static
- cold silence
- roads that do not quite connect

Avoid:

- horror gore
- cruelty-focused scenes
- despair-heavy writing
- bleak endings in early content

## Visual Mood

Preferred visual cues:

- hearthlight
- soft cloth banners
- warm stone
- lanterns
- stitched borders
- yarn-like trails
- readable silhouettes
- cute but practical equipment

The UI should look like a crafted object from Purralia, not a generic fantasy overlay.

## Sound Mood

Sound must carry the cozy identity:

- purr ambience
- bell collar details
- soft loot chimes
- tactile clicks
- cloth and thread Foley
- short cat syllables for voice
- adaptive music that can stay pleasant during long idle sessions

## Writing Voice

Use short, clear lines for gameplay text. Use richer prose for region descriptions and story beats.

Good example:

> The Great Hearth flickers for the first time in a hundred winters. Somewhere below Pawmont, a thread has come loose.

Bad example:

> The ultimate darkness has awakened and all hope is lost.

## Acceptance Check

Before adding any story, UI, reward, enemy, or region detail, ask:

- Does it still feel warm?
- Does it make Purralia more specific?
- Does it keep The Unravel meaningful?
- Does it support sound and cuteness as pillars?
- Does it avoid dark-pattern pressure?
